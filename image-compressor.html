How to compress an image

  1. Choose or drop an image.
  2. Set quality — 70–80% is the sweet spot: much smaller files, no visible difference for web and sharing.
  3. Optionally cap the width (1280px suits blogs and listings).
  4. Download the compressed JPG.

Why size matters

A phone photo can be 4–8 MB. Compressed to ~300 KB it uploads 15× faster, uses less mobile data, and pages carrying it load quicker — page speed is a Google ranking factor and a direct reason visitors stay or leave. Output is always JPEG, which also converts PNG photos to a much smaller file (transparency is flattened onto white).

Will compression ruin my photo's quality?

At 75–85% quality, the difference is invisible on screens for normal photos. For printing or archiving, keep your original file as well — compression here is for sharing and web use.

Is my image uploaded to a server?

No. The image is processed with your browser's canvas on your own device. You can verify this by loading the page, going offline, and compressing — it still works.
